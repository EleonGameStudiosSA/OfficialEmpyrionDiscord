<div class="section">
<div class="rule">
<h3>How to Power a Base</h3>
<p>
                Every base requires a working power source. You can power a base using solar,
                fuel, or both together.
            </p>
<div class="subsection">
<h4>Available Power Sources</h4>
<ul>
<li><strong>Option A:</strong> Up to <strong>15 Solar Panels</strong> with at least <strong>1 Solar Capacitor</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Option B:</strong> Fuel Tanks containing fuel with at least <strong>1 Generator</strong> turned on.</li>
<li><strong>Option C:</strong> Use both systems together for maximum efficiency.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<div class="rule">
<h3>How Power Is Used</h3>
<p>
                When both solar and fuel generators are available, the game follows a specific priority order.
            </p>
<div class="subsection">
<h4>Power Priority</h4>
<ol>
<li>Solar Panels generate power while the sun is available.</li>
<li>If solar production exceeds the current base usage, the Solar Capacitor stores the excess energy.</li>
<li>If the base requires more power than the Solar Panels can provide, Generators automatically supply only the missing power.</li>
</ol>
</div>
<div class="note">
<strong>Important:</strong> Your battery may appear to never charge because solar power first reduces fuel consumption before excess energy is stored.
            </div>
</div>
<div class="rule">
<h3>Generator Capacity</h3>
<div class="subsection">
<h4>Avoid Generator Overload</h4>
<p>
                    You must have enough generators enabled to support your base's total power demand.
                    If generators are overloaded, they will take damage.
                </p>
<p>
                    Charged Solar Capacitors also count as available generator capacity, increasing the total power your base can safely provide.
                </p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="rule">
<h3>Common Misconceptions</h3>
<div class="subsection">
<h4>A) "My battery never charges, so solar doesn't work."</h4>
<p>Solar power is always used first. Stored battery power is consumed before fuel is burned.</p>
</div>
<div class="subsection">
<h4>B) "Leaving the generator on wastes fuel."</h4>
<p>False. If solar fully covers your base's power needs, the generator burns no fuel.</p>
</div>
<div class="subsection">
<h4>C) "More generators or larger generators consume more fuel."</h4>
<p>False. Fuel consumption depends only on the remaining power demand after solar has contributed.</p>
</div>
<div class="subsection">
<h4>D) "Generators should charge solar batteries."</h4>
<p>They do not. Solar Capacitors only store energy produced by Solar Panels.</p>
</div>
<div class="subsection">
<h4>E) "Single Player doesn't collect solar while I'm away."</h4>
<p>It does. The game simulates elapsed time for solar charging, fuel usage, plant growth, and other systems while you're away.</p>
</div>
<div class="subsection">
<h4>F) "Turning off base power stops battery charging."</h4>
<p>False. Solar Panels will continue charging the Solar Capacitor even when the base power switch is turned off.</p>
</div>
<div class="subsection">
<h4>G) "Sitting on the generator prevents freezing."</h4>
<p>This one is actually true—but you'll eventually die from heat or radiation instead.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="note">
<strong>Best Practice:</strong>
            Leave your generators enabled alongside your solar system. Solar automatically handles as much of the load as possible,
            reducing fuel consumption, while generators seamlessly cover any additional power your base requires.
        </div>
</div>
